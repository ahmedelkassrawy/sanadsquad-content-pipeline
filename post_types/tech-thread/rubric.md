---
criteria: [hook, technical_accuracy, grounding, voice, clarity, cta]
---
Score each configured criterion from 1 to 5 with a concrete reason that points to the text. Check every factual claim against exactly the supplied evidence, including uncited claims. Unsupported claims and off-brand language fail closed. Explain every rejection.

What the scores mean: 5 is ready to post as it is, 3 is acceptable but plain, 1 fails the criterion.

- hook: 5 when the first tweet states a specific vulnerable pattern or contrast that makes a developer want to read on; 3 when clear but generic, or when it opens with a question; 1 when vague or clickbait.
- technical_accuracy: 5 when mechanism, conditions and mitigation match the evidence exactly, keeping compiler versions and chain conditions; 3 when correct but simplified in a way that changes no claim's truth or conditions; 1 when wrong or misleading. Dropping a version or condition that the evidence attaches to a claim makes that claim broader than its evidence: score 1 and set unsupported_claim. Read every code span as a reviewer would: a call missing an argument (an ERC-20 `transfer` with no recipient), code that would revert or not compile in the library it names, or code from one project presented as working in another scores 2 or lower. Recompute every worked example from the thread's own numbers: a total that does not add up (a vault that "holds 101 tokens" after a 100-token deposit on top of 101), or a reference to a figure or example the thread never showed ("instead of 20_000e6"), scores 1 for technical_accuracy, even when the source says the same. Check every formula against the evidence: a flipped ratio or a different operand order (for example writing assets over shares where the evidence divides shares by assets) scores 1.
- grounding: 5 when every factual clause traces to the cited evidence_text; 3 when one minor wording stretch adds no new fact; 1 when any fact, statistic or source is invented. A paraphrase that keeps a supported fact's meaning and conditions is grounded and is not a stretch.
- voice: 5 for the calm, precise engineering voice of the brand voice; 3 when slightly promotional or loose, or when the same authority is credited in more than one tweet ("OWASP says", "Per OWASP"), or tweets carry section labels ("Root cause:", "Closing check:"); 1 for hype, unsupported certainty, banned wording, or text that points at the research material ("the article's example", "from the discussion"). A thread that mostly reorders sentences from evidence_text instead of explaining them in its own words (several tweets reuse evidence sentences nearly verbatim) scores 2.
- clarity: 5 when each tweet makes one point and the thread builds from hook to mechanism, example, mitigation and a closing check; 3 when understandable but repetitive; 1 when the order is confusing or jargon is left unexplained. A tweet that announces code or a list it does not contain scores 2 or lower. A thread that restates its opening point or any earlier point in a later tweet scores 2.
- cta: 5 when the thread ends with a concrete check or next step the reader can apply, supported by the evidence; 3 for a generic closing; 1 for no closing, or a closing that adds an unsupported claim.

Hard-fail flags:
- unsupported_claim: true when any factual clause is not supported by the supplied evidence, or drops a version or condition the evidence attaches to it, even if every other clause is.
- off_brand: true for a banned word or phrase, hype, a promise of complete protection, metadata leaked into the published text (source ids, field names).
